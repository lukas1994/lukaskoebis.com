# Lukas writing style sheet

Derived from homepage bio, Health, Mountain King (2026), Scaling (2022), and the Wat Pa Tam Wua draft + review edits. Use this for every draft and rewrite. Review / edit freely.

## Voice
- First person, direct. Sounds like talking to a smart friend, not giving a TED talk.
- Curious and concrete. Prefer what happened / what you tried over abstract claims.
- Light humour and understatement OK (`Those evenings were hard to beat`, `probably enlightened`). Don’t force jokes.
- Comfortable saying “I don’t know”, “I think”, “not sure I’d manage it”, “It sounds quite confusing…”.
- British spelling when it comes up: *summarises*, *favourite*, *labour*, *practise* (verb) / *practice* (noun).

## Sentence & rhythm
- Write complete sentences. Don’t drop articles, subjects, or verbs for a telegram style (`Next months:` → `In the next months I want to…`; `Closest Western cousin` → `The closest Western cousin`).
- Mix short punchy lines with longer explanatory ones. Don’t leave important claims as fragments.
- Em dashes for asides — like this.
- Contractions: *I'm*, *don't*, *wouldn't*, *it's*.
- Italics for emphasis on the load-bearing word, not whole clauses.
- Finish lists cleanly. Prefer `podcasts, YouTube, and news` over trailing `…` / `, ...`.
- Prefer plain English over insider jargon when writing for a general reader (`helping people` over `loving-kindness` / `metta` unless the Pali term is the point).

## Structure
- Open with the thing itself (what you did / built / experienced), not a throat-clearing preamble.
- Sections with plain `h2` titles — not clever marketing headers.
- Lists when the content is genuinely list-shaped (schedule, precepts, checklist). Otherwise prose.
- When a doctrine list has a nested list (e.g. Eightfold Path under Magga), fold the nested one into the parent item instead of stacking two bullet blocks.
- Specific numbers and names beat vague adjectives (`8 days`, `Route 1095`, `60min walking`).
- One idea per stretch: don’t make the same point in two sections.
- Topic transitions need a full bridging sentence (`Unique mental states aside…`, `The way you're supposed to know for yourself is practice.`).
- Don’t promise a topic in a bridge sentence if the next section is something else (e.g. don’t lead with “science” into a doctrine section).
- Prefer `exercise` over `work out` in this post’s register; `helping people` over Buddhist jargon in checklists.
- End with an open invitation or next step when it fits — short, not salesy. Prefer `Please reach out if…` over clipped `Reach out if…` when the close is a real ask.
- Footnotes: use a superscript link to a Notes section at the end of the post — not a grey aside mid-flow.

## Terminology (Buddhist posts)
- On Theravada material, prefer Pali spellings: *Dhamma*, *kamma*, *Dhamma talk*.
- Sanskrit forms (*Dharma*, *karma*) are what most Western readers know — footnote once early.
- Be consistent within a post.

## Attribution / claims
- When summarising a book’s thesis, prefer `He's arguing that…` over `The bit that clicked is…` unless you’re clearly stating your own takeaway.
- Don’t present contested claims as proven.

## Words / habits to keep
- `—` asides
- Parenthetical asides `(motion sickness pills recommended!)`
- `e.g.` when giving one example among several
- Full subject lines: `This is what I like most…`, `The closest…`, `When I'm back in London…`
- Concrete verbs: `work out`, `physical labour`, `helping people`

## Words / habits to avoid
- Trailing ellipses in lists or transitions (`…`, `, ...`)
- Soft calibration padding stacks: *pretty*, *basically*, *kind of* (occasional *quite* in speech-like lines is fine)
- LLM tells: *delve*, *tapestry*, *landscape* (metaphorical), *robust*, *leverage*, *unlock*, *journey* (unless literal), *the full stack*
- *It's worth noting that*, *In today's world*, *At its core*
- Symmetrical three-part slogans that sound generated
- Over-hedging stacks: `It could perhaps be said that…`
- Moralizing wrap-ups; let the experience land
- `BCE` → use `BC`
- Don’t smooth away blunt opinions
- Clipped checklist jargon (`fewer sensual defaults`)
- Half-finished transitions that jump topics cold
- Mid-page grey “note on spelling” blocks — use a real footnote instead

## Quick before/after tests
1. Would this sentence survive if someone removed every adjective? If yes, keep it.
2. Does it sound like something you’d say out loud at dinner? If no, cut or rewrite.
3. Is there a concrete noun (place, time, tool, book) nearby? If not, add one.
4. Did you already say this earlier in the post? If yes, cut or merge.
5. Can you read the sentence aloud without mentally inserting missing words? If you have to fill gaps, rewrite it.
6. Does the next paragraph need a bridge, or does it jump topics cold?
